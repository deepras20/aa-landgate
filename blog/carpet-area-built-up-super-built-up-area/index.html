What Is Carpet Area, Built-Up Area
& Super Built-Up Area? Explained Simply

When you walk into a builder’s office in Mumbai, you will hear three numbers thrown at you — carpet area, built-up area, and super built-up area — often in a way that makes the flat seem much larger than it actually is. Understanding what each term means could easily save you ₹5–10 lakhs on your next property purchase.

What Is Carpet Area — and Why It Matters Most

Carpet area is the net usable floor area inside your flat — the space where you can actually lay a carpet. It includes all rooms (bedrooms, living room, kitchen, toilets), internal walls, and staircases within the flat. What it excludes is everything outside the door of your apartment: the lobby, corridor, staircase, lift shaft, and common amenities.

Under RERA (Real Estate Regulation & Development Act, 2016), every builder in Maharashtra is required to quote prices on the basis of carpet area only. This is the number that tells you the real size of the home you are paying for — and the one you should always ask for first. In Mumbai’s market, a 2 BHK advertised as 950 sq ft typically has a carpet area of 650–720 sq ft — roughly 70–76% of the larger figure. That gap represents real money at current rates of ₹8,500–₹14,000 per sq ft across Mira Road East, Thane, and Navi Mumbai.

What Is Built-Up Area?

Built-up area includes the carpet area plus the thickness of the internal and external walls of your flat, plus the balcony or terrace exclusive to your unit. Think of it as the floor plate your flat actually occupies in the building, walls and all. Built-up area is typically 10–15% higher than carpet area. So a flat with a 700 sq ft carpet area usually has a built-up area of around 770–800 sq ft.

This measurement was commonly used in older property sales before RERA standardised pricing norms. You may still encounter it in resale transactions, so it is important to know what it covers.

What Is Super Built-Up Area?

Super built-up area — also called saleable area — is built-up area plus your proportionate share of all common areas in the building: the entrance lobby, lifts, staircases, generator room, clubhouse, gym, and any other shared amenity. The ratio of common area added to each flat is called the loading factor, and it typically ranges from 20–35% in Mumbai projects.

This means a flat with a 700 sq ft carpet area could be sold as a 980–1,050 sq ft super built-up area flat. Super built-up area was the standard sales metric before RERA, and while it is now illegal to price new units on super built-up area, you will still encounter this figure in pre-RERA resale properties, brochures, and casual conversation.

RERA’s Game-Changing Rule: Price by Carpet Area

Since MahaRERA came into force in Maharashtra, every registered new project must clearly state the carpet area in the sale agreement and all marketing material. The price must be quoted per square foot of carpet area. This one rule transformed how buyers in Mumbai can compare properties.

Before RERA, two builders with identical carpet-area flats could quote wildly different sizes by applying different loading factors. Today, if you are buying a MahaRERA-registered flat anywhere in Maharashtra — Mira Road, Kharghar, Thane, Panvel, or Andheri — you are legally protected from carpet-area misrepresentation. Always verify a project’s RERA registration on the MahaRERA website (maharerait.maharashtra.gov.in) before signing anything.

How to Calculate Real Cost: A Mira Road Example

Let’s say a builder in Mira Road East quotes ₹10,500 per sq ft on a 950 sq ft flat — a headline price of ₹99.75 lakh. Ask for the RERA carpet area: it may be only 690 sq ft. That means you are actually paying ₹10,500 × 950 = ₹99.75 lakh for 690 sq ft of liveable space — an effective carpet-area rate of ₹14,456 per sq ft.

Now compare this with a neighbouring project quoting ₹13,500 per sq ft on 700 sq ft of carpet area (total: ₹94.5 lakh). The second project is cheaper overall and gives you slightly more usable space for less money. Without understanding these three measurements, you would have chosen the first option based on the headline price alone.
